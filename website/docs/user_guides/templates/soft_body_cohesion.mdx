Rapier supports three ways of holding the particles of a soft-body together: shape matching, constraints, and the
Finite Elements Method (FEM). They can be combined, e.g., shape matching on top of edge constraints. Note that shape
matching works on the particles alone, whereas the constraints need edges or cells, and the FEM solver needs cells.

### Shape matching
Shape matching doesn't need any element. At each timestep, the rest shape of the body is placed where it best fits its
current shape, i.e., both shapes are given the same center of mass, and the rest shape is given the rotation bringing
its particles the closest to the current ones. Each particle is then pulled toward its twin in that matched rest shape
by a spring:

<center>
![Shape-matching steps](/img/soft_bodies/shape_matching.svg)
</center>

This is cheap, and a body always recovers its original shape whatever the deformation it went through. On the other
hand, the particles don't interact with their neighbors: pushing on one particle doesn't pull the ones around it, which
makes the deformations feel very local. Shape matching is enabled by
<notjs>`SoftBodyBuilder::shape_matching`</notjs><js>`SoftBodyDesc.setShapeMatching`</js><c>the `shapeMatching` field of the description</c><py>`SoftBodyBuilder.shape_matching`</py>, and the strength of its
springs is the **shape matching softness** (<notjs>`shape_matching_softness`</notjs><js>`shapeMatchingSoftness`</js><c>`shapeMatchingSoftness`</c><py>`shape_matching_softness`</py>)
of the [material](./soft_bodies.mdx#constraints):

:::tip
Use shape matching for low-detail deformations, or whenever computing a topology (edges, cells) isn't desired. It is
enabled by default by the <notjs>`trimesh` and `polyline` constructors</notjs><js>`trimesh` and `polyline` constructors</js><c>`r3SoftBodyDesc_SetSurfaceMesh` and `r2SoftBodyDesc_SetTrimesh` constructors (unless the `shapeMatching` override disables it)</c><py>`trimesh` constructor</py>. Note however that it performs very poorly for ropes,
cloth, or any open shape. Also note that combining it with edges makes the deformations spread to the neighbors to look
more realistic.
:::

### Constraints
The constraints-based soft-body solver is the default solver<notjs> (`SoftBodySolver::Constraints`)</notjs><c> (`R3_SOFT_SOLVER_CONSTRAINTS`)</c><py> (`SoftBodySolver.CONSTRAINTS`)</py>, and the most versatile one:
every edge and cell element of the deformation lattice becomes a constraint, solved together with the contacts and the joints of the scene.

<center>
![Edge and cell constraints](/img/soft_bodies/spring_constraints.svg)
</center>

An edge is a spring-damper pulling its two particles back toward its rest length. A cell is either a volume constraint
keeping its area or its volume, the shape itself being held by the edges, or an elastic element resisting any
deformation. This is selected by the **cell model** of the body
(<notjs>`cell_model`</notjs><js>`setCellModel`</js><c>`cellModel`</c><py>`cell_model`, which takes a `SoftBodyCellModel`</py>):
- <notjs>`Volume`</notjs><js>`Volume`</js><c>`R3_SOFT_CELL_VOLUME`</c><py>`VOLUME`</py>: one constraint per cell keeping its area (2D) or its volume (3D) at its rest value. This is the cheapest
  model, and combined with the edges it is often enough to obtain a convincing jelly.
- <notjs>`Corotational`</notjs><js>`Corotational`</js><c>`R3_SOFT_CELL_COROTATIONAL`</c><py>`COROTATIONAL`</py>: linear elasticity expressed in the rotation-free frame of the cell. It is stable at any stiffness and
  recovers from inverted cells.
- <notjs>`NeoHookean`</notjs><js>`NeoHookean`</js><c>`R3_SOFT_CELL_NEO_HOOKEAN`</c><py>`NEO_HOOKEAN`</py>: stable Neo-Hookean hyperelasticity. It feels stiffer than linear elasticity on compression, but softer on
  tension.

The stiffness of every element is configured by the <notc>`SoftBodyMaterial`</notc><c>`R3SoftBodyMaterial`</c> of the body, which can be given to the <notc>builder</notc><c>description</c> or
set at any time with <rapier>`SoftBody::set_material`</rapier><bevy>the component of the same name, which wraps the
Rapier material (re-exported as `RapierSoftBodyMaterial`) and overrides the one of the
builder</bevy><js>`SoftBody.setMaterial`</js><c>`r3SoftBody_SetMaterial` (the current one being given by `r3SoftBody_Material`)</c><py>the `material` property of `SoftBody`</py>.<c> A material is initialized by `r3DefaultSoftBodyMaterial`, or by `r3UniformSoftBodyMaterial` which gives the same softness to every constraint.</c><py> This property is a live view of the material of the body: modifying one of its fields modifies the body, whereas `SoftBodyMaterial.copy` gives a detached copy. A material is created with the `SoftBodyMaterial` constructor, which takes any of its fields as keyword arguments, or with `SoftBodyMaterial.uniform` which gives the same softness to every constraint.</py> The edges and the volume constraints are given a softness, i.e., a
natural frequency (in Hz) and a damping ratio instead of a stiffness, so it doesn't depend on the masses of the
particles:
- The **edge softness** (<notjs>`edge_softness`</notjs><js>`edgeSoftness`</js><c>`edgeSoftness`</c><py>`edge_softness`</py>) for the structural edges;
- The **bend softness** (<notjs>`bend_softness`</notjs><js>`bendSoftness`</js><c>`bendSoftness`</c><py>`bend_softness`</py>) for the bending edges and the
  dihedral constraints;
- The **volume softness** (<notjs>`volume_softness`</notjs><js>`volumeSoftness`</js><c>`volumeSoftness`</c><py>`volume_softness`</py>) for the volume constraints.

The elastic cells are given a Young modulus (<notjs>`young_modulus`</notjs><js>`youngModulus`</js><c>`youngModulus`</c><py>`young_modulus`</py>, in force per unit
area in 3D, per unit length in 2D), a Poisson ratio (<notjs>`poisson_ratio`</notjs><js>`poissonRatio`</js><c>`poissonRatio`</c><py>`poisson_ratio`</py>), and a
damping ratio (<notjs>`elastic_damping_ratio`</notjs><js>`elasticDampingRatio`</js><c>`elasticDampingRatio`</c><py>`elastic_damping_ratio`</py>) instead. Their natural frequency
is derived from these, therefore a body meshed more finely doesn't become stiffer, whereas it becomes more expensive to
simulate.

Finally, a body with a closed surface can preserve the area (2D) or the volume (3D) it encloses
(<notjs>`volume_preservation`</notjs><js>`setVolumePreservation`</js><c>`volumePreservation`</c><py>`volume_preservation`, or `enable_volume_preservation` after the insertion</py>), which target can be scaled by a
<rapier>`volume_factor`</rapier><bevy>`volume_factor` (or a `SoftBodyVolumeFactor` component)</bevy><js>`setVolumeFactor`</js><c>`volumeFactor` (or `r3SoftBody_SetVolumeFactor` after the insertion)</c><py>`volume_factor` (or the `volume_factor` property of `SoftBody` after the insertion)</py>
greater than 1 in order to inflate  the body, e.g., to simulate a pressurized blob:

:::info
The stiffness effectively simulated by the constraints solver depends on its convergence: with too few iterations, a stiff
body looks softer than its material says. This is why soft-bodies are configured with 3 additional internal PGS solver iterations by default, which can
 be modified with
<notjs>`additional_pgs_iterations`</notjs><js>`setAdditionalPgsIterations`</js><c>`additionalPgsIterations`</c><py>`additional_pgs_iterations`</py>. The whole island a body belongs to
can also be given additional substeps with
<notjs>`additional_solver_iterations`</notjs><js>`setAdditionalSolverIterations`</js><c>`additionalSolverIterations`</c><py>`additional_solver_iterations`</py>, just like
[rigid-bodies](./rigid_bodies.mdx#solver-settings).

<center>
![The same chain solved with 1, 4, and 32 iterations](/img/soft_bodies/stiffness_iterations.svg)
</center>
:::

The following table gathers the settings to look at for the most common problems:

| Problem | What to change |
|---------|----------------|
| The body is too soft, or stretches too much. | Raise the natural frequency of the material's <notjs>`edge_softness`</notjs><js>`edgeSoftness`</js><c>`edgeSoftness`</c><py>`edge_softness`</py>, or its <notjs>`young_modulus`</notjs><js>`youngModulus`</js><c>`youngModulus`</c><py>`young_modulus`</py> for the elastic cells. Give it more <notjs>`additional_pgs_iterations`</notjs><js>`setAdditionalPgsIterations`</js><c>`additionalPgsIterations`</c><py>`additional_pgs_iterations`</py>, or switch it to the FEM solver with <notjs>`solver`</notjs><js>`setSolver`</js><c>`solver`</c><py>`solver`</py>. |
| A cloth stretches, but should still fold easily. | Keep a stiff <notjs>`edge_softness`</notjs><js>`edgeSoftness`</js><c>`edgeSoftness`</c><py>`edge_softness`</py>, and give it a soft <notjs>`bend_softness`</notjs><js>`bendSoftness`</js><c>`bendSoftness`</c><py>`bend_softness`</py>. |
| A rope compresses like a spring. | Make its edges resist stretching only with <notjs>`tension_only`</notjs><js>`setTensionOnly`</js><c>`tensionOnlyEdges`</c><py>`tension_only`</py>. |
| The body keeps wobbling after an impact. | Raise the <notjs>`damping_ratio`</notjs><js>`dampingRatio`</js><c>`damping_ratio`</c><py>`damping_ratio`</py> of the material's softnesses and its <notjs>`elastic_damping_ratio`</notjs><js>`elasticDampingRatio`</js><c>`elasticDampingRatio`</c><py>`elastic_damping_ratio`</py>, or its <notjs>`deformation_damping`</notjs><js>`deformationDamping`</js><c>`deformationDamping`</c><py>`deformation_damping`</py> (which damps the deformations but not the motion of the body as a whole). |
| A closed body collapses, or must be inflated. | Enable <notjs>`volume_preservation`</notjs><js>`setVolumePreservation`</js><c>`volumePreservation`</c><py>`volume_preservation`</py>, and give it a <notjs>`volume_factor`</notjs><js>`setVolumeFactor`</js><c>`volumeFactor`</c><py>`volume_factor`</py> greater than 1. |
| The deformations are too local. | Combine <notjs>`shape_matching`</notjs><js>`setShapeMatching`</js><c>`shapeMatching`</c><py>`shape_matching`</py> with edges, or rely on edges and cells alone. |

### FEM solver
The FEM solver (<rapier>`SoftBodySolver::Fem`, behind the `fem` cargo feature</rapier><bevy>`SoftBodySolver::Fem`,
behind the `fem` feature of `bevy_rapier`, given to the builder or with the `SoftBodyElasticitySolver`
component</bevy><js>`SoftBodySolver.Fem`</js><c>`R3_SOFT_SOLVER_FEM`, given to the `solver` field of the description or to `r3SoftBody_SetSolver`, and requiring the `fem` feature of the library</c><py>`SoftBodySolver.FEM`, given to the builder with `solver`, or to the `solver` property of `SoftBody` after the insertion</py>) resolves the elasticity of the whole body at once and semi-implicitly:
the forces and the stiffness of every cell are assembled into a single linear system, solved at each substep. Therefore
the stiffness of the body no longer depends on the number of solver iterations, which makes it capable of simulating
very stiff materials, as well as more realistic plastic deformations and failures:

<center>
![Assembly of the FEM system](/img/soft_bodies/fem_system.svg)
</center>

This comes at a price: the system is factorized at each timestep, and every constraint touching the body (contacts,
joints) needs a solve against it. Note that the FEM solver requires cells, so it only applies to the bodies built with
cells, e.g., with the <notjs>`grid`, `cuboid`, or `volumetric`</notjs><js>`grid`, `cuboid`, or `volumetric`</js><c>`r2GridSoftBodyDesc`, `r3CuboidSoftBodyDesc`, or `r3VolumetricSoftBodyDesc`</c><py>`cuboid`, `volumetric`, or `volumetric_with`</py> constructors. The configuration of its linear solves is shared
by every body using it, and lives in the [integration parameters](./integration_parameters.mdx#soft_bodies):

The linear solves stop at the relative residual
<notjs>`linear_tolerance`</notjs><js>`softBodiesFemLinearTolerance`</js><c>`linearTolerance` (set by `r3FemSetLinearTolerance`)</c><py>`linear_tolerance`</py>, or after
<notjs>`max_linear_iterations`</notjs><js>`softBodiesFemMaxLinearIterations`</js><c>`maxLinearIterations` (set by `r3FemSetMaxLinearIterations`)</c><py>`max_linear_iterations`</py> conjugate-gradient iterations,
whatever the residual. The bodies with at most
<notjs>`max_dense_dofs`</notjs><js>`softBodiesFemMaxDenseDofs`</js><c>`maxDenseDofs` (set by `r3FemSetMaxDenseDofs`)</c><py>`max_dense_dofs`</py> degrees of freedom (`600` by default) are
factorized directly, whereas the larger ones rely on the iterative conjugate gradient.

:::tip
Use the FEM solver for stiff materials which simulated stiffness must not depend on the iteration count, e.g., the
chassis of a car or a metal beam. This also results in more realistic plasticity and tearing.
:::
