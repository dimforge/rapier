A few settings are shared by every soft-body of the world. They are part of the [integration
parameters](./integration_parameters.mdx#soft_bodies)<bevy> (the `integration_parameters.soft_bodies` field of the
`RapierContextSimulation` component)</bevy><c> (the `softBodies` field of `R3IntegrationParameters`)</c><py> (the `soft_bodies` property of `IntegrationParameters`, a `SoftBodiesSettings`)</py>, so they can
be changed between two steps:
- The **re-sweep strain**
  (<notjs>`resweep_strain`</notjs><js>`softBodiesResweepStrain`</js><c>`r3SoftBodiesSetResweepStrain`</c><py>`resweep_strain`</py>) is the strain
  beyond which a constraint is solved once more after the contacts of every substep. This is what keeps a light body
  buried under heavier ones from being torn apart by them.
- The **maximum number of extra substeps**
  (<notjs>`max_extra_substeps`</notjs><js>`softBodiesMaxExtraSubsteps`</js><c>`r3SoftBodiesSetMaxExtraSubsteps`</c><py>`max_extra_substeps`</py>) is
  the number of substeps a soft-body is allowed to ask for while it is hit fast. Those substeps  bound the motion of its
  particles, and the motion of the rigid-bodies approaching them, by about one particle radius per substep. Giving zero
  disables them.
- The **contact stiffening**
  (<notjs>`contact_stiffening`</notjs><js>`softBodiesContactStiffening`</js><c>`r3SoftBodiesSetContactStiffening`</c><py>`contact_stiffening`</py>)
  multiplies the natural frequencies of the contacts involving a soft-body. Those contacts run stiffer than the rigid
  ones because the mass behind one contact is the mass of a few particles, and not the mass of the whole body.

In addition, the detection of the penetrations and of the tangles, as well as the way the bodies recover from them, are
configured by the **recovery** settings (<notjs>`recovery`, a `SoftRecoverySettings`</notjs><js>`softBodiesRecovery`,
a `SoftRecoverySettings`</js><c>`recovery`, an `R3SoftRecoverySettings`</c><py>`recovery`, a `SoftRecoverySettings`</py>), where every mechanism can be switched
off individually (see the [contacts](./soft_bodies.mdx#penetrations-and-tangles) section for the ones to look at
first). The tuning of the linear solves of the [FEM solver](./soft_bodies.mdx#fem-solver) lives there as well (<notjs>`fem`, a
`SoftFemParameters`</notjs><js>the `softBodiesFem*` parameters</js><c>`fem`, an `R3SoftFemParameters`</c><py>`fem`, a `SoftFemParameters`</py>):

<c>

Each of these settings has its own getter and setter, named after its field: e.g., `r3SoftBodiesResweepStrain` and
`r3SoftBodiesSetResweepStrain`, `r3RecoveryCrossingRepulsion` and `r3RecoverySetCrossingRepulsion`, or
`r3FemLinearTolerance` and `r3FemSetLinearTolerance`. All of them can also be read at once with
`r3IntegrationParameters`, which returns a copy of the integration parameters of the world: that copy is modified, then
written back with `r3SetIntegrationParameters`. Note that the `fem` field, as well as the `r3Fem*` functions, only
exist when the library is built with the `fem` feature:

The `soft_bodies` property of the integration parameters of the world is a live view of the settings, and so are its
`recovery` and `fem` properties: setting one of their fields changes the world directly. Assigning a whole
`SoftBodiesSettings` replaces them all, and their `copy` method gives a detached copy, e.g., to keep them aside before
an experiment:
