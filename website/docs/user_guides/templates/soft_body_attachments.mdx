A particle can also be attached directly to a rigid-body (<rapier>`SoftBody::attach_particle`</rapier><bevy>the
`SoftBodyAttachments` component</bevy><js>`SoftBody.attachParticle`</js><c>`r3SoftBody_AttachParticle`</c><py>`SoftBody.attach_particle`</py>), which is
the simplest way of combining the two kinds of bodies: a rope tied to a swinging ball, a flag attached to its pole,
etc. Unlike pinning, an attachment is a two-way point-to-point constraint: the rigid-body holds the particle, and the
particle pulls the rigid-body back. The anchor is the position the particle has when the attachment is created,
expressed in the local frame of the rigid-body. Note that a particle attached twice keeps both of its attachments, and
that an attachment is undone with
<rapier>`SoftBody::detach_particle`</rapier><bevy>its removal from the `SoftBodyAttachments` component (removing the
component detaches every particle)</bevy><js>`SoftBody.detachParticle`</js><c>`r3SoftBody_DetachParticle`</c><py>`SoftBody.detach_particle`</py>:

Each entry of the `SoftBodyAttachments` component attaches one particle to the entity of a
rigid-body (or of a [cluster](./soft_bodies.mdx#clusters)). Each time the component changes, the attachments of the
soft-body are updated to match it: the attachments that didn't change keep their anchor, and the ones targeting an
entity which rigid-body isn't created yet are retried on the next frames:

Note that `r3SoftBody_DetachParticle` returns whether the particle was attached at all.

:::info
The target of an attachment must be an ordinary rigid-body: `r3SoftBody_AttachParticle` rejects the root body and
the cluster proxies of a soft-body (they are rigid-bodies too, see `r3RigidBody_IsSoftFrame`). Two soft-bodies are
linked with a joint between their [soft frames](./soft_bodies.mdx#soft-frames-joints-and-rigid-colliders) instead.
:::

Note that the rigid-body set of the world is given to `SoftBody.attach_particle`, so that the anchor can be expressed in
the local frame of the rigid-body. The attachments of a soft-body are listed by its `particle_attachments` property,
each `SoftParticleAttachment` giving its `particle`, its rigid-body (`body`), its `local_anchor`, and the `impulse` it
applied during the last step.
