The state of a soft-body is the state of its particles, which are identified by their index in the body. Their positions
and their velocities can be read (<notjs>`particle_position`, `particle_positions`, `particle_velocity`,
`particle_velocities`</notjs><js>`particlePosition`, `particlePositions`, `particleVelocity`,
`particleVelocities`</js><c>`r3SoftBody_ParticlePosition`, `r3SoftBody_ParticlePositions`, `r3SoftBody_ParticleVelocity`, `r3SoftBody_ParticleVelocities`</c><py>`particle_position`, `particle_positions`, `particle_velocity`, `particle_velocities`</py>) and modified (<notjs>`set_particle_position`,
`set_particle_velocity`</notjs><js>`setParticlePosition`, `setParticleVelocity`</js><c>`r3SoftBody_SetParticlePosition`, `r3SoftBody_SetParticleVelocity`</c><py>`set_particle_position`, `set_particle_velocity`</py>) at any time, one by one or all at
once. The elements built from them (<notjs>`edges`, `cells`, and `boundary`</notjs><js>`edges`, `cells`, and
`boundary`</js><c>`r3SoftBody_Edges`, `r3SoftBody_Cells`, and `r3SoftBody_Boundary`</c><py>`edges`, `cells`, and `boundary`</py>) can be read as well, e.g., in order to render the body with your own mesh.<c> These arrays are copied into a buffer of your own, which capacity is given as the last argument, and a `NULL` buffer with a zero capacity only returns the length of the array. The elements are given as flat arrays of particle indices: 2 per edge, 3 (2D) or 4 (3D) per cell, and 2 (2D) or 3 (3D) per boundary element.</c><py> The arrays of every particle and of every element are NumPy arrays with one row per particle or per element, e.g., of shape `(N, 3)` for the positions and `(B, 3)` for the boundary triangles. All the positions (or velocities) are modified at once by assigning such an array to `particle_positions` (or `particle_velocities`). A snapshot of all the properties of a particle (its mass, its rest position, whether it is pinned, etc.) is given by `particle`.</py><bevy> These
are methods of the Rapier soft-body of the entity, given by the `soft_body` and `soft_body_mut` methods of the
physics context (see [soft-bodies and entities](./soft_bodies.mdx#soft-bodies-and-entities)). Note that the positions
are expressed in world-space, and not in the frame of the entity.</bevy>

A particle can also be **pinned** (<rapier>`set_particle_pinned`</rapier><bevy>the `SoftBodyPinnedParticles`
component</bevy><js>`setParticlePinned`</js><c>`r3SoftBody_SetParticlePinned`</c><py>`set_particle_pinned`</py>). A pinned particle is kinematic: it is no longer affected by the forces
nor by the contacts, and it will simply hold its position, or follow the kinematic target
(<rapier>`set_particle_kinematic_target`</rapier><bevy>the `SoftBodyKinematicTargets`
component</bevy><js>`setParticleKinematicTarget`</js><c>`r3SoftBody_SetParticleKinematicTarget`</c><py>`set_particle_kinematic_target`</py>) or the velocity it is given. This is, e.g., how a piece of cloth
is hung on a wall, or how a rope is dragged by the player. Releasing the particle gives it back its nominal mass and
lets it keep its current velocity:

:::warning
Setting the position of a particle explicitly teleports it: no contact is taken into account along the way, so a
particle can be moved inside of another object this way. Whenever the motion must be seen by the contacts and by the
friction (to drag a piece of cloth, for example), it is recommended to pin the particle and to give it a kinematic
target instead.
:::

### Controlling a region kinematically
A whole region of the body is controlled at once through a [cluster](./soft_bodies.mdx#clusters) covering it. Pinning
the cluster (<rapier>`set_cluster_pinned`</rapier><bevy>the `SoftBodyClusterPinned`
component</bevy><js>`setClusterPinned`</js><c>`r3SoftBody_SetClusterPinned`</c><py>`set_cluster_pinned`</py>) pins all of its particles, and its kinematic target
(<rapier>`set_cluster_kinematic_target`</rapier><bevy>the `SoftBodyClusterKinematicTarget` component, a world-space
`Transform` which also pins the cluster when it is inserted</bevy><js>`setClusterKinematicTarget`</js><c>`r3SoftBody_SetClusterKinematicTarget`</c><py>`set_cluster_kinematic_target`</py>) moves them
rigidly: each pinned particle is sent where the rest shape of the cluster places it at the target pose, with the
matching velocity. The rest of the body is then simulated as usual, and drags behind the controlled region, e.g., the
hand of a soft character carrying something:
